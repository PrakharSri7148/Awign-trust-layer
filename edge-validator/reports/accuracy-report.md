# ML model accuracy report

Generated by `backend/tools/accuracy_report.py` (`cd client && npm run report`). Same inputs give the same numbers (seeded; latency excluded). **Every table is labelled REAL (photos someone captured) or SYNTHETIC (generated); synthetic numbers are never real-world accuracy.**

Thresholds in force: `thresholds.json` sha256 `85128bed4e96` (history: `edge-validator/thresholds-changelog.md`).

## 0. Read this first

### Weakest areas (ranked)

1. **Liveness / anti-spoofing is unmeasured on real data.** Without `live_selfies/` and `spoof_*` samples there is no real APCER/BPCER; the thresholds are starting values.
2. **Sensor fusion has no real trace behind it.** Every motion/network number is SYNTHETIC (section 5b); the thresholds in `fusion.json` are uncalibrated starting values. Record traces into `Sensor-fusion/samples/`.
3. **The fraud classifier has never seen real data.** Its accuracy is a property of the simulator (section 5b); a shifted world already costs it noticeable accuracy.
4. **Replayed motion on a frozen mock location and wrong-area networks are only partly caught**: 138/200 = 69% (95% CI 62%-75%) (SYNTHETIC, by construction the hardest spoofs).
5. **No real positive examples for:** Exposure: over-exposed, Lens obstruction, Glare, Noise / compression. They are validated on synthetic degradations only, which cannot show how real photos of these problems behave.
6. **Face check false-reject rate on clear faces is unknown** (no `good_selfies/`), and the face check can block a face-required task.
7. **Shake (motion blur) misses real issues**: recall 79/120 = 66% (95% CI 57%-74%) (REAL).
8. **The `good/` set is stock photography**, sharper and more uniform than worker phone photos, so false-flag rates on real worker photos are probably higher than shown.
9. No fairness slice evidence (skin tone, lighting, head coverings, device) exists in any section; the samples carry no such tags.

### What this report does and does not tell you

- **Does:** how each check behaves on the photos currently in `edge-validator/samples/`, with intervals that show how much (or little) 10s of photos can support; where the errors are, by file name; how fragile the fraud model's synthetic assumptions are.
- **Does not:** how accurate the system is on your workers' real submissions. The `good/` set is stock photos, several issue types have no real examples, the fraud model and any synthetic positive come from our own generators, and liveness has no data yet.
- Small samples (under 30 per class) are marked *indicative only*; a Wilson interval on 0 errors out of 100 still allows ~3.7%.
- 'REAL' means the files exist and were labelled by their folder; it does not mean the labelling was audited.

### Synthetic sets (provenance: SYNTHETIC)

Generated by `tools/calibrate.py synthesize` (seeded) from 40 evenly spaced REAL good photos: `lens_covered` (40), `noisy_compressed` (40), `overexposed` (40), `glare` (40). Each synthetic positive is a degraded copy of a photo that is also in the REAL good set, so the design is *paired* and optimistic: it proves the check reacts to the degradation it was written for, not that real photos with that problem are caught.


## 2. Photo-quality checks as classifiers

Each check is treated as a detector of one issue. **Positive** = a photo from the issue folder; **negative** = a photo from `good/` (REAL stock photos, see the inventory). **Flagged** = the check returned *warn* or *fail*; **fail level** = *fail* only (for blur, exposure, resolution, obstruction and face a fail blocks the photo). Intervals are 95% (Wilson for rates, bootstrap for AUC). The operating point is the one currently in `thresholds.json`.

### Leakage and validity notes

- Training rows and every 'test' set below come from the SAME hand-written generator (generate_synthetic_data.py). The score distributions, the class overlap and the labels are all assumptions chosen by the authors, not measurements.
- Therefore high accuracy here shows only that gradient boosting can learn the generator's rules. It says nothing about how often real fraud is caught or how often honest workers are flagged.
- The rule 'a poor photo tips AMBIGUOUS submissions towards review' is encoded in the labels. The photo-quality feature only matters because the generator says so; it was not learned from real reviewed cases.
- motion_score and ambient_score are produced by the generator too: their real-world distributions (and how a real GPS spoofer looks) are unknown.

### Leakage and validity notes

- The simulator and the detectors were written by the same people. The gait frequency (1.8 Hz), the hand-tremor size, the GPS error model (AR(1) noise, accuracy 1-2x sigma), the share of carrier gateways that resolve far away and the whole scenario list are assumptions, not measurements.
- The scores are produced by the REAL engines from simulated raw streams (the simulator holds no thresholds or weights), but a detector that is right about the simulated world can still be wrong about real phones.
- Training rows and the 'in distribution' test rows come from the same simulator and seed. The different-seed set only changes the random draws; the shifted-world set changes the world (noisier GPS, weaker steps, more tremor, lower sensor rate, more far carrier gateways).
- Fraud labels are by scenario: a 'fraud' row is fraud because the simulator made it a spoof. Real spoofing apps, real emulators and real fraud patterns are unknown.
- The rule 'with the same weak evidence a poor photo tips a case to review' (the `ambiguous` pair) is an ASSUMPTION encoded in the labels, as in the old generator; it was not learned from reviewed cases.
- Replayed real accelerometer data on a frozen mock location is, by construction, invisible to the motion rules; only the 'too perfect GPS' sign and the network can see it. The `fraud_hard` group shows how often that is enough.
- The IP-context rows are simulated; the real DB-IP databases were not used for any number here.
